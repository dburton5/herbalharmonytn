## 🌿 The Six Herbs, One by One

### Peppermint Leaf

Peppermint is the bright, cooling flavor you taste first. Its main natural compound, **menthol**, helps relax the smooth muscles of the digestive tract. That's why peppermint tea has been a go-to after heavy meals for a long time.

**What it can support:** digestive comfort, occasional bloating, and that "too full" feeling after dinner. It also makes the whole cup taste fresh and easy to drink.

### Mullein Leaf

Mullein is the herb that started Herbal Harmony. Its soft, fuzzy leaves have been used in traditional herbalism for the throat and lungs for centuries. The leaves contain **mucilage**, a gel-like substance that coats and soothes.

**What it can support:** throat comfort and easier breathing during cold and allergy season. Modern research on mullein is still limited, so think of it as a time-tested tradition rather than a proven treatment.

### Cinnamon

Cinnamon brings warmth and a natural sweetness, so most people don't need to add sugar. It's rich in **antioxidant polyphenols**. Researchers have studied cinnamon and blood sugar, but most of those studies use concentrated supplements, and the results are mixed.

**What it can support:** a satisfying, naturally sweet cup that helps you cut back on sugary drinks, which is a real win on its own.

### Ginger Root

Ginger is one of the best-studied herbs in the world. Its active compounds, **gingerols**, have solid research behind them for easing nausea and helping the stomach empty more smoothly.

**What it can support:** settling an upset stomach, easing occasional nausea, and adding a warming feeling that's especially nice on cold mornings.

### Turmeric Root

Turmeric gives the tea its golden color. Its best-known compound, **curcumin**, has been studied a great deal for its effect on the body's inflammation response.

Here's the honest part: most of that research uses concentrated curcumin supplements, and curcumin doesn't dissolve well in water. A cup of tea delivers a small amount. That doesn't make it pointless, but it does mean turmeric here is a gentle daily contributor, not a high-dose treatment.

**What it can support:** a small, steady dose of plant antioxidants as part of an overall healthy routine.

### Bladderwrack

Bladderwrack is a brown seaweed, and it's the "touch of the sea" in this blend. Like other sea vegetables, it naturally contains **iodine** and trace minerals. Your thyroid uses iodine to make the hormones that help regulate energy and metabolism.

**What it can support:** a natural source of iodine and minerals. Because iodine affects the thyroid, please read the safety notes below if you have a thyroid condition.

## 🌿 Put Together, What Can a Cup Do?

No single cup of tea is a miracle. Where herbal tea shines is in **consistency**. One to three cups a day, week after week, can become a small habit that adds up. Here's what Harmony Herbal Blend is built to support:

- **Digestion:** peppermint and ginger are a classic pairing for an easy, comfortable stomach after meals.
- **Seasonal comfort:** mullein and the warmth of ginger and cinnamon make it a soothing cup during cold and allergy season.
- **Hydration:** every cup counts toward your daily fluids, and a tea you actually enjoy makes drinking more water easier.
- **A caffeine-free ritual:** no jitters and no afternoon crash. You can drink it in the morning or right before bed.
- **Fewer sugary drinks:** with cinnamon's natural sweetness, it's a simple swap for soda or sweet tea.
- **A quiet moment for yourself:** measuring, steeping, and sipping slowly is a few minutes of calm in a busy day. That matters too.

## 🌿 What It Won't Do

I'd rather tell you this up front. Harmony Herbal Blend will not cure a disease, replace your medication, "detox" your organs, or make you lose weight on its own. Your liver and kidneys already handle detox for you. A good tea supports a healthy lifestyle. It doesn't replace one.

If anyone ever tells you an herbal tea can do all of that, that's your cue to walk away.

## 🌿 How to Brew It

1. **Shake the jar first.** Fine pieces of turmeric and ginger settle to the bottom, so a quick shake keeps every cup even.
2. **Measure about 2¾ teaspoons** into a fine-mesh infuser or tea bag.
3. **Pour 8 oz of freshly boiled water** over the herbs.
4. **Steep 10 to 15 minutes.** A longer steep pulls out more from the roots and leaves.
5. **Strain well and enjoy.** A fine-mesh strainer matters here, because mullein leaves have tiny hairs that can tickle your throat.

Add a little raw honey or a squeeze of lemon if you like. Enjoy 1 to 3 cups a day.

## 🌿 Good to Know Before You Sip

Herbs are plants, but plants are powerful, so please check with your doctor or healthcare provider first if you:

- Have a **thyroid condition** or take thyroid medication (because of the iodine in bladderwrack)
- Are **pregnant or nursing**
- Take **blood thinners** or **diabetes medication**
- Have **gallbladder issues**
- Deal with frequent **heartburn or acid reflux**, since peppermint can make it worse for some people
